# V06 — Compose an experience across media

Let artists compose relationships among sound, movement, animation, lighting,
and audience interaction. Time and behavior become shared material that can
be heard, seen, and manipulated through different instruments.

## Experience

An artist draws an accelerating spiral, gives its angular speed to a musical
voice, and maps its radius to light intensity. A graph exposes those mappings
with units and ranges. They stretch a phrase, pin a beat, and bend a transition
while listening and watching a spatial preview. During performance, keyboard
commands select phrases and alternative mappings; a temporary gesture returns
cleanly to the authored score when released or cancelled.

The horizon includes interactive installations, generative performance,
expressive games, and compositions that respond to people without losing
their authored structure. The same relationship could have several sensory
interpretations rather than belonging to a single track type.

## What the combination enables

Plots serve as envelopes and trajectories, math describes modulation, graphs
route meaning, and 3D supplies a stage or another output. Structured editing
makes the relationships inspectable; tactile interaction makes them playable.
The composition records explicit conversions between time, distance, pitch,
and intensity. A shared numeric value is insufficient to establish a meaningful
cross-media relationship. Audio timing needs its own execution contract.

## First question to test

Can an artist revise one expressive phrase across two media without maintaining
two independent timelines? Build a local piece linking one motion path and one
sound envelope. Allow precise keyboard timing edits and continuous pointer
preview, with one committed revision. Compare this with manually synchronizing
separate curves. Record timing drift and whether the coupling expands expression
or mostly produces accidental correlations.

## Assessment and uncertainty

Agency **4/5** · Expression **5/5** · Transfer **4/5**.
Probe cost **High**: audio scheduling, timing, and expressive mapping are new
boundaries. External devices, live collaboration, and immersive output remain
separate expansions.

Potential confidence: **Moderate**. Artists producing intentional results and
reusing a phrase in a different medium would raise confidence; rigid mappings
or interaction latency that disrupts performance would lower it.
