# V11 — Turn patterns into structures

Let makers move between a repeated rule, a flat pattern, and the structure it
creates. The medium connects symbolic composition with material craft and
spatial behavior.

## Experience

A maker defines a folding motif, repeats it across a sheet, and previews the
resulting surface. Change a crease family and see the global shape respond;
select a troublesome region in 3D and return to its flat pattern. A graph
exposes adjacency, an expression describes repetition, and a curve records
opening angle along a chosen path. Keyboard traversal follows folds and pattern
groups, supporting exact edits as well as tactile manipulation.

The larger vision includes woven structures, knitting patterns, modular
assemblies, paper mechanisms, and acoustic or decorative surfaces. A pattern
could carry its rules, assembly interpretation, and editable variations.

## What the combination enables

Structured notation represents generative rules, a canvas organizes motifs,
and 3D shows the consequences of a declared construction model. Plots are useful
when they expose a meaningful parameterized behavior; they need not be present
in every craft. The mapping between a pattern and a physical structure is
domain-specific. A geometric preview cannot establish material strength,
manufacturability, or reliable folding by itself.

## First question to test

Can a maker predict and intentionally revise global form through a local rule?
Use one known foldable motif family, a small repeated patch, and a bounded
kinematic model. Compare edits in the flat and folded views, including invalid
parameters. Have the maker build a paper sample and note where the preview
misleads. General origami solving, fabrication hardware, and automatic toolpaths
are outside this first question.

## Assessment and uncertainty

Agency **4/5** · Expression **4/5** · Transfer **3/5**.
Probe cost **High**: a credible construction model, coordinated picking, and
physical comparison are needed. Each additional craft introduces its own rules.

Potential confidence: **Low**. A successful physical sample and understandable
rule revisions would strengthen it; frequent gaps between the model and craft
practice would argue for a narrower instrument or a different domain.
