# V07 — Learn by changing a system

Create learning environments in which a learner can manipulate a phenomenon,
form a prediction, and discover how its symbolic and spatial descriptions fit
together. Teachers author experiments and explanations in the same medium.

## Experience

A learner pulls a pendulum, watches its path, and asks what controls its period.
They change length, predict an outcome, and compare runs. Selecting part of the
trajectory reveals the corresponding interval on a plot and term in an
expression. A teacher can expose a simplifying assumption and let the learner
find where it breaks. Keyboard controls reach every meaningful variable and
measurement, including exact values and repeatable experiments.

The horizon extends to probability, mechanics, geometry, algorithms, and
systems thinking. Lessons can grow from playful exploration into formal work
without discarding the objects the learner already understands.

## What the combination enables

Simulation gives experience, plots reveal patterns, structured math introduces
precision, and graphs organize related concepts or experiments. The same
selection can connect these views without asserting that every representation
is equivalent. An authored lesson controls when notation appears, but learners
retain a route to inspect it. Smooth previews, meaningful resistance, and
repeatable keyboard actions make hypothesis testing feel direct.

## First question to test

Does coordinated manipulation help a learner transfer understanding to a new
case? Build one oscillator lesson with prediction, manipulation, and comparison.
Use a small fixed model and a bounded expression display/editor. Test a new
parameter combination afterward, asking for explanation rather than recognition.
Include a keyboard-only route. Teacher authoring can initially be explicit
configuration; a complete curriculum platform is unnecessary for this question.

## Assessment and uncertainty

Agency **4/5** · Expression **4/5** · Transfer **5/5**.
Probe cost **Medium**: lesson structure, coordinated representations, and an
appropriate comprehension test need design. Broad subject coverage would need
many authored models and pedagogical decisions.

Potential confidence: **Moderate**. Improved prediction and explanation on
unseen examples would strengthen it; engagement without transferable
understanding would weaken it. No learning benefit is established by this brief.
