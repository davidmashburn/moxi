# V02 — Compose worlds through rules

Give world builders a way to shape environments and the rules that animate
them together. Worlds can be fictional ecosystems, games, kinetic installations,
or deliberately simplified social simulations.

## Experience

Sketch islands and currents, scatter a species, and draw its response to light.
Connect predators, food, and seasonal events in a relationship graph. Stretch
a migration path and watch a ghost preview of the next season; a constraint
marks where the proposed behavior exceeds the model's movement budget. Branch
the world, change one rule, and compare the emerging patterns side by side.
Keyboard focus moves through regions, entities, and rules without depending
on a precise 3D pick.

Eventually a world could become a playable explanation, a fictional setting,
or an instrument for composing emergent behavior. Authors would work at the
level of rules and consequences as well as individual objects.

## What the combination enables

Geometry defines habitat; graphs define interaction; expressions define rules;
plots expose response functions, distributions, and changes over time. A curve
is an input when it specifies a rule and an output when it records a run.
Different views retain the identity of the same species, region, or event.
Tactile feedback makes spatial and mathematical limits perceptible while an
author edits. A compelling simulation is still an authored model, with no
automatic claim that it predicts a real ecosystem or society.

## First question to test

Can authors intentionally produce an interesting pattern instead of only
discovering accidents? Build one local 2D world with two interacting populations,
one editable response curve, and a repeatable seed. Let an author make a
prediction, revise the rule, and compare outcomes. Test whether the graph and
curve help them understand effects that take time to emerge. A small fixed
simulation is sufficient; a general world engine is not a prerequisite.

## Assessment and uncertainty

Agency **4/5** · Expression **5/5** · Transfer **4/5**.
Probe cost **Medium**: simulation stepping, deterministic branching, and useful
rule editing need integration. General worlds add major domain and scale work.

Potential confidence: **Moderate**. Authors explaining and reproducing desired
patterns would raise confidence; visually attractive but opaque outcomes would
lower it. Domain validity remains separate from authoring quality.
