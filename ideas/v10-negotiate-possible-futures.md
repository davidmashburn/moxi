# V10 — Negotiate possible futures together

Give groups a shared medium for expressing different goals, exploring their
consequences, and constructing alternatives without erasing disagreement.
Participants can manipulate preferences as well as model parameters.

## Experience

A group designs a fictional neighborhood. One participant draws an acceptable
walking-distance envelope; another prioritizes quiet space; another pins a
budget. A map shows candidate layouts while plots reveal the tradeoffs. Each
participant can keep a distinct preference profile. The group branches a
proposal, changes one assumption, and records why it accepts a compromise.
Keyboard users can inspect and change every constraint in a structured list,
with the map acting as a coordinated view.

The horizon includes participatory design, organizational planning, cooperative
games, and household decisions. A shared decision could retain its alternatives,
uncertainties, and unresolved differences as editable material.

## What the combination enables

Mind maps retain alternatives, spatial views ground consequences, plots expose
tradeoffs, and math makes chosen calculations inspectable. Reactive feedback
supports negotiation while people are still expressing intent. Preferences,
physical constraints, and model predictions have different meanings. No hidden
weighted objective decides what is fair; any aggregation is an explicit group
choice. An attractive frontier does not validate a model of human outcomes.

## First question to test

Can a small group reach a better-understood compromise while retaining the
minority view? Use a local shared screen and a fictional allocation problem
with three preferences and a finite set of candidates. Ask participants to
explain what changed, who benefited, and what remains disputed. Compare with
a static alternatives table. Networking and sophisticated optimization are
unnecessary for testing whether the interaction supports negotiation.

## Assessment and uncertainty

Agency **4/5** · Expression **4/5** · Transfer **4/5**.
Probe cost **Medium**: explicit preference ownership, branching, and interpretable
tradeoff views are required. Distributed collaboration and real policy models
would add separate technical and domain work.

Potential confidence: **Low**. Participants understanding each other's tradeoffs
would strengthen it; false consensus induced by defaults or opaque aggregation
would undermine it. This is a proposal for deliberation, not automated judgment.
