# V03 — Invent a notation that becomes a tool

Let people create an expressive language for their domain and immediately use
it to think, compose, calculate, and communicate. The product is a studio for
making usable notations with meaning, editing behavior, and live consequences.

## Experience

A choreographer defines a phrase as a path, duration, orientation, and relation
to another performer. They assemble phrases into a visual score, edit a timing
curve, and preview the movement. Selecting a symbol reveals its structured
meaning; changing that meaning updates its instances. Keyboard entry follows
the notation's slots and groups, with discoverable completion and reversible
structural edits. A second author can learn and modify the score without
opening the notation's implementation.

The horizon reaches circuit descriptions, textile patterns, scientific diagrams,
musical systems, and languages for domains that currently live in improvised
spreadsheets or drawings. Sharing a notation would share its tools and examples.

## What the combination enables

Structured editing supplies syntax; canvas composition supplies spatial grammar;
reactivity connects meaning to simulation; plots and 3D supply domain-specific
views. The author declares which properties a mark represents and which edits
are valid. A symbol library alone does not supply those semantics. Mathematical
content retains its exact editable text/layout correspondence; other domain
languages need their own explicit serialization and round-trip contracts.

## First question to test

Can a domain author make a small notation another person can actually use?
Support three phrase types through a constrained declarative schema, without
arbitrary plugins or live code replacement. Have one author define a sequence,
grouping rule, and timing handle, then ask another to complete an unfamiliar
revision. Measure authoring complexity, discoverability, and whether the user
can predict the result of a structural edit.

## Assessment and uncertainty

Agency **5/5** · Expression **5/5** · Transfer **5/5**.
Probe cost **High**: even a restricted notation needs grammar, meaning, editing,
and understandable authoring tools. General language extensibility is larger.

Potential confidence: **Low**. This offers the largest expressive horizon, but
could collapse into a difficult language toolkit. Successful use by a second
author, followed by a second substantially different notation, would materially
raise confidence. Needing framework expertise for every new symbol would lower it.
