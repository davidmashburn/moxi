# V05 — Make arguments spatial and executable

Give authors and readers a medium where claims, evidence, assumptions, and
models remain connected as an argument changes. A reader can investigate the
conditions under which a conclusion holds.

## Experience

An author explains why one transport proposal might outperform another. The
reader selects a conclusion, follows its supporting claims, and adjusts an
assumption about demand. Computed results update; qualitative claims remain
explicitly qualitative. Dragging a threshold reveals where a conclusion changes.
The reader creates an alternative branch, annotates a disputed premise, and
returns to the original argument with one command. Keyboard traversal follows
the reasoning structure as well as the document's reading order.

The horizon includes scientific explanations, investigative journalism,
design rationales, and public debate that retains inspectable models instead
of freezing every result into a picture.

## What the combination enables

Text carries interpretation, graphs carry dependencies, math carries formal
claims, plots expose sensitivity, and spatial views situate consequences.
These representations let readers examine different parts of an argument
without losing its context. Dependency does not imply causation, and a model
result does not prove its premises. Authored evidence, assumptions, and derived
results have distinguishable roles and provenance.

## First question to test

Does manipulation improve comprehension or merely make a persuasive story more
engaging? Author a small argument with two assumptions, one quantitative model,
and one explicit limitation. Ask readers to find a condition that reverses its
conclusion and explain why. Compare their understanding with a static version.
The probe needs no collaborative editor, external data connector, or universal
proof system; it does need an honest representation of unsupported claims.

## Assessment and uncertainty

Agency **4/5** · Expression **3/5** · Transfer **4/5**.
Probe cost **Medium**: claim structure, provenance, and linked narrative editing
are new integration work. Open publication and collaborative argument revision
would add substantial scope.

Potential confidence: **Moderate**. Readers identifying assumptions and
counterexamples would strengthen it; increased confidence without increased
understanding would undermine the premise. This ranks on expressive opportunity,
not a claim that interactive arguments are inherently more truthful.
