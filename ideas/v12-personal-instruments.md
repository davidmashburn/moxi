# V12 — Make interfaces into personal instruments

Let people reshape how they operate a creative tool around their own movement,
attention, habits, and precision needs. Authoring the interface becomes a
practical activity within the tool, with clear meanings and reversible changes.

## Experience

A musician wants to control a phrase with a small number of reliable actions.
They gather meaningful operations into a personal instrument, draw the response
curve for one continuous control, and arrange a keyboard sequence for moving
between phrases. Another person uses the same musical model through a spatial
surface. Each can preview a remapping, inspect the operation it will invoke,
and return to a known configuration without losing the composition.

The horizon includes creative tools adapted to individual motor preferences,
specialized expert workflows, and interfaces that can evolve as tasks or
abilities change. A [camera-based hand instrument](v15-hand-pose-control.md)
explores one concrete continuous input route; its tracking and motor-control
questions have their own experiment.

## What the combination enables

Graphs describe action sequences and modes, plots define transfer functions,
math expresses precise mappings, and native controls or spatial surfaces give
them usable forms. Application operations retain their meaning across those
representations. Smooth feedback helps a person tune a mapping, while keyboard
focus and ordinary editing remain stable. A prettier arrangement of existing
buttons alone would not achieve this vision.

## First question to test

Can a user create a personally useful interaction mapping without becoming an
application developer? Offer a fixed set of operations in one simple creative
task, two input routes, a response curve, and an explicit mode graph. Observe
whether users can build, explain, and recover their configuration. Include
people whose interaction preferences differ; do not substitute a developer's
simulated needs for their experience. Custom hardware is unnecessary initially.

## Assessment and uncertainty

Agency **5/5** · Expression **4/5** · Transfer **5/5**.
Probe cost **Medium**: an understandable mapping editor and reliable focus/mode
semantics are needed. General adaptation across arbitrary applications is a
much larger interoperability problem.

Potential confidence: **Low**. Independently authored configurations that users
keep using would strengthen it; fragile modes, frequent accidental commands,
or continual expert assistance would weaken it. This brief makes no medical
or universal accessibility outcome claim.
